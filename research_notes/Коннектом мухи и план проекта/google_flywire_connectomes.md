# Google Connectomics, FlyWire and the Drosophila connectome landscape (state as of September 2026)

Scope of these notes: (a) Google Research Connectomics team's fly work and adjacent tools; (b) the FlyWire whole-brain connectome and its 2024 Nature package plus the 2025-2026 BANC follow-on; (c) which datasets/tools are practically reusable for a connectome-constrained simulation of the fly visual system + whole CNS. All dates are release/publication dates as found in the sources.

---

## Key Question 1: Which Drosophila connectome datasets exist, how do they differ, and which are best for a visual-system + whole-CNS simulation?

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 there are five synapse-resolution adult Drosophila datasets in active use: FlyWire/FAFB (female brain, v783, Oct 2024), hemibrain (female half central brain, 2020), Janelia optic lobe v1.1 (male right optic lobe, Apr 2024 / Nature Mar 2025), MaleCNS v1.0 (male brain + both optic lobes + VNC, Jun 2026 / Cell Sep 2026), and BANC v888 (female brain + VNC, May 2026 / Nature Jun 2026). For a visual-system + whole-CNS simulation the two "complete CNS" options are MaleCNS (FIB-SEM, fully proofread, CC-BY, best-annotated optic lobes with column IDs) and BANC (female, ssTEM, CC-BY, FlyWire/CAVE ecosystem); FlyWire v783 remains the most tool-rich and most-simulated brain-only dataset.

### Cited Findings

**FlyWire / FAFB (female adult fly brain)**
- The flagship paper "Neuronal wiring diagram of an adult brain" (Dorkenwald et al., Nature, Oct 2, 2024) presents 5 × 10^7 chemical synapses between 139,255 neurons reconstructed from an adult female Drosophila melanogaster, with annotations of cell classes/types, nerves, hemilineages and predicted neurotransmitters — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y); [Nature collection "The FlyWire connectome"](https://www.nature.com/collections/hgcfafejia)
- Companion paper "Whole-brain annotation and multi-connectome cell typing of Drosophila" (Schlegel et al., Nature 634:139–152, online Oct 2, 2024): 8,453 annotated cell types, of which 3,643 were previously proposed in the hemibrain and 4,581 are new; nearly all hemibrain neurons could be matched morphologically in FlyWire, but about one-third of hemibrain-proposed cell types could not be reliably re-identified — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07686-5); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11446831/)
- Companion paper "Neuronal parts list and wiring diagram for a visual system" (Matsliah et al., Nature 2024): completes the list of cell types intrinsic to the optic lobe and their connectivity rules; Codex maps cell types to locations in the hexagonal lattice of columns and ommatidia; each eye has ~800 ommatidia mapping to lamina columns arranged in a hexagonal lattice — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07981-1) (paywalled; details taken from search-result abstracts)
- "Network statistics of the whole-brain connectome of Drosophila" (Lin et al., Nature 2024) is the fourth core paper; analysis code at murthylab/flywire-network-analysis — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07968-y); [GitHub](https://github.com/murthylab/flywire-network-analysis)
- FlyWire Consortium: members from >146 labs at 122 institutions; citizen scientists proofread 18,000 neurons and annotated >38,000 — [Cambridge Neuroscience](https://neuroscience.cam.ac.uk/49701-2/); ~54.5 million synapses cited in the Princeton press release — [Princeton](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2024/10/02/mapping-entire-fly-brain-step-toward-understanding-diseases-human-brain)
- FlyWire blog (Dec 2024): the connectome contains "over 127,000 proofread cells, ~73,000 of which are in the optic lobes"; unicolumnar cell types (L1–L5, Mi1, Mi4, Mi9, Tm1, Tm2, Tm9, Tm20, Dm2, Dm8, T1, T4a–d, T5a–d, C2, C3, R7, R8) have roughly 790 instances per hemisphere — [FlyWire blog](https://blog.flywire.ai/2024/12/20/optic-lobe-tag-rally/)
- Codex lists the current FAFB snapshot as v783 (October 2023), 139,255 neurons — [Codex FAQ](https://codex.flywire.ai/faq); [Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/)
- Synapse detection: Codex states that from July 2025 onward the synapse table uses the method of Yu et al. ("Princeton synapses"), replacing Buhmann et al./Heinrich et al. detections; the Yu et al. preprint "New Synapse Detection in the Whole-Brain Connectome of Drosophila" (bioRxiv, July 2025) trained new networks with iteratively generated ground truth for problem cases (dark cytosol, axo-axonic, polyadic synapses; JONs, photoreceptors) — [Codex About](https://codex.flywire.ai/about_flywire); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.11.664377v1)

**Hemibrain (Janelia FlyEM + Google, 2020)**
- Released Jan 22, 2020; ~25,000 neurons, >20 million synaptic connections, FIB-SEM at 8×8×8 nm, a 26-trillion-voxel volume covering roughly half of the (female) central brain; access via neuPrint and Neuroglancer — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/releasing-the-drosophila-hemibrain-connectome-the-largest-synapse-resolution-map-of-brain-connectivity/)

**Janelia optic lobe v1.1 (male right optic lobe)**
- Version 1.1 released April 2024; >50,000 neurons and >700 types (≈150 intrinsic types/~16,000 cells; >90 connecting types/~32,000 cells; ~350 projection types/~4,500 cells; >100 centrifugal types/~280 cells); neuPrint dataset `optic-lobe:v1.1`; flat-file downloads from a public Google Cloud bucket; neuprint-python / neuprintr; licensed CC-BY — [Janelia Optic Lobe page](https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/optic-lobe)
- Paper: Nern et al., "Connectome-driven neural inventory of a complete visual system", Nature, published March 26, 2025: ~53,000 neurons classified into 732 types; about half newly named; fewer than 100 types account for most cells and connections — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08746-0); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12119369/); Cell Type Explorer — [reiserlab](https://reiserlab.github.io/male-drosophila-visual-system-connectome/index.html); code — [GitHub](https://github.com/reiserlab/male-drosophila-visual-system-connectome-code)
- Codex also mirrors this dataset as "MAOL v1.1" (52,445 neurons) — [Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/)

**MaleCNS (male brain + optic lobes + VNC; Janelia FlyEM + Cambridge + MRC LMB + Google)**
- First finished connectome of an entire male Drosophila CNS (central brain, both optic lobes, ventral nerve cord, intact neck connective), FIB-SEM, same specimen as the optic lobe v1.1 release; v0.9 initial release Oct 5, 2025; v1.0 June 8, 2026 (minor proofreading, annotation refinement); CC-BY 4.0 — [MaleCNS release notes](https://male-cns.janelia.org/release/); [Janelia MaleCNS page](https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome)
- Google blog (Sept 3, 2026): "Sexual dimorphism in the complete connectome of the Drosophila male central nervous system" published in Cell; 166,000+ neurons, 125 million synaptic connections; "largest brain map by number of neurons to date" — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/); [Cell](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00942-6)
- 262 sex-specific and 114 sexually dimorphic cell types (4.8% of central brain); neuPrint dataset `male-cns:v1.0`; Clio, Cell Type Explorer, NeuronBridge; downloads of image volumes, annotations, connectivity tables — [Janelia MaleCNS page](https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome)
- Codex mirrors it as "MCNS v1.0" (166,700 neurons) — [Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/)
- Berg et al. (2025) supplemental repo includes optic-lobe column assignments (L1, R7, R8 neurons with column type classifications, Excel) and male–female aligned edges comparing MaleCNS vs FlyWire cell-type weights — [GitHub flyconnectome/2025malecns](https://github.com/flyconnectome/2025malecns)
- Companion Cell 2026 paper "The organization of visual pathways in the Drosophila brain" — [Cell](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00941-4)

**BANC (female Brain-And-Nerve-Cord; Harvard/Lee lab + FlyWire/Princeton + Zetta)**
- ~188,000 neurons and 199 million predicted synapses from one adult female, spanning brain, SEZ, cervical connective and entire VNC; imaged at 4 nm in-plane by serial-section EM (GridTape TEM); segmented by Zetta AI; annotated for cell type, neurotransmitter, hemilineage, behavioral function and cross-dataset identity; static snapshot v888; CC BY 4.0; paper Bates et al. 2026 "Distributed control circuits across a brain-and-cord connectome", Nature (DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10735-w; preprint bioRxiv 2025.07.31.667571v3) — [GitHub htem/BANC-project](https://github.com/htem/BANC-project); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.31.667571v2.full.pdf)
- Production timeline: sample prep 2020, sectioning/imaging 2021, alignment 2022, automated reconstruction 2023, proofreading/annotation 2024, analysis 2025 — [BANC wiki](https://github.com/jasper-tms/the-BANC-fly-connectome/wiki/)
- Codex lists BANC v888 (May 20, 2026; 158,262 neurons in Codex) and v626 (July 20, 2025) — [Codex FAQ](https://codex.flywire.ai/faq); [Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/). Note the discrepancy: Codex's 158,262 vs the paper's ~188,000 (Codex counts likely reflect proofread/filtered cells only).
- Access: flywire.ai/banc_access, codex.flywire.ai/banc, Neuroglancer at ng.banc.community/view, GCS bucket gs://lee-lab_brain-and-nerve-cord-fly-connectome, Harvard Dataverse DOI 10.7910/DVN/7WTH1N — [FlyWire blog Nov 3, 2025](https://blog.flywire.ai/2025/11/03/the-banc-brain-and-nerve-cord/); [BANC-project](https://github.com/htem/BANC-project)

**Other related datasets**
- MANC (male adult nerve cord, v1.2.1, 23,665 neurons in Codex) and FANC (female VNC, Azevedo et al. Nature July 2024) exist as VNC-only datasets — [Codex](https://codex.flywire.ai/); [Zetta AI publications](https://zetta.ai/)
- Google/Janelia earlier FAFB automated segmentation (2019 blog): FAFB imaged as thousands of 40-nm TEM sections, >40 trillion pixels — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/an-interactive-automated-3d-reconstruction-of-a-fly-brain/)

### Inferences
- For a *visual system + whole CNS* simulation, MaleCNS v1.0 is the only dataset that has both optic lobes, central brain and VNC fully proofread in one specimen with FIB-SEM isotropic resolution and explicit column assignments; BANC is the female equivalent but its optic lobes are less emphasized in the published materials found (its paper focuses on brain–cord control circuits), so optic-lobe completeness in BANC should be verified before relying on it.
- FlyWire v783 has the richest downstream ecosystem (Codex visual-column tools, flyvis-compatible types, Shiu LIF model, Loihi port, Eon embodied model), so a pragmatic path is: prototype on FlyWire (brain only, with flyvis for the optic lobe), then port to MaleCNS or BANC for descending/VNC motor output.
- Cross-dataset cell-type matching (Schlegel 2024 FlyWire↔hemibrain; Berg 2025 MaleCNS↔FlyWire aligned edges) makes mixing datasets feasible at the cell-type level.

## Key Question 2: What exactly did Google contribute (segmentation, tools) versus Janelia/Princeton/Zetta?

### Takeaway
Google's role across all fly datasets is automated 3D reconstruction (flood-filling networks, plus SECGAN/local realignment for FAFB and the newer PATHFINDER system for MaleCNS) and infrastructure (Neuroglancer, TensorStore, and involvement in CAVE); Janelia did imaging, staining, synapse detection and proofreading (hemibrain, optic lobe, MaleCNS); Princeton/Seung lab built FlyWire's proofreading platform and community, with Zetta AI providing segmentation for BANC and other Seung-lab-linked datasets.

### Cited Findings
- Google's connectomics team formed in 2014 in collaboration with Janelia; FFNs were developed by 2018; hemibrain released 2020; H01 preprint 2021 (Science 2024: ~57,000 cells, 150 million synapses in ~1 mm^3 of human cortex, 1.4 PB raw); TensorStore open-sourced 2022; key technologies listed as FFN, SegCLR, TensorStore, Neuroglancer and CAVE — [Google Research blog "Ten years of neuroscience at Google"](https://research.google/blog/ten-years-of-neuroscience-at-google-yields-maps-of-human-brain/)
- FAFB reconstruction (blog Aug 5, 2019; paper "Automated Reconstruction of a Serial-Section EM Drosophila Brain with Flood-Filling Networks and Local Realignment", with HHMI Janelia and Cambridge): thousands of Cloud TPUs ran FFNs over >40 trillion pixels; local realignment estimated slice-to-slice consistency; SECGAN (Segmentation-Enhanced CycleGAN) synthesized replacement imagery for missing sections; released with Neuroglancer — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/an-interactive-automated-3d-reconstruction-of-a-fly-brain/)
- Hemibrain: Google's FFNs reduced required human proofreading "from tens of millions to hundreds of thousands of hours"; Janelia FlyEM developed staining/tissue-slicing, did VR-assisted manual proofreading and trained synapse-detection networks — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/releasing-the-drosophila-hemibrain-connectome-the-largest-synapse-resolution-map-of-brain-connectivity/)
- MaleCNS (Sept 2026 blog): Google contributed FFNs, the PATHFINDER reconstruction system, synthetic neuron training data, and Neuroglancer; Janelia led the project and did human proofreading — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/)
- FlyWire (Princeton): developed at Princeton with US BRAIN Initiative grants (MH117815, MH129268, U24 NS126935 to Murthy and Seung); cell segments generated by AI, proofread by community; synapses auto-detected (Buhmann/Heinrich, then Yu et al. from July 2025); hierarchical annotations from Schlegel et al. (Jefferis lab); NT predictions from Eckstein & Bates et al. — [Codex About](https://codex.flywire.ai/about_flywire)
- Zetta AI (Seung-lab spin-out) provides automated segmentation, synapse detection and proofreading tooling; performed 3D segmentation for BANC; lists FlyWire (Dorkenwald 2024), FANC, MICrONS and BANC among its publications — [Zetta AI](https://zetta.ai/); [BANC search summary](https://github.com/htem/BANC-project)
- Google's Neural Mapping site (2026) lists: male fruit fly CNS (166,000 neurons, Gerry Rubin/Janelia lead), larval zebrafish "Fish1" automated whole-brain map (2026; Fish2 human-verified in progress), ZAPBench (2025, ICLR; >70,000 neurons activity forecasting), H01 (2021), mouse hippocampus (2023 start, 2–3% of mouse brain over 5 years, NIH-supported), tools FFN, LICONN, MoGen (morphology generation via point-cloud flow matching), Neuroglancer; researchers Viren Jain, Michał Januszewski — [Google Neural Mapping](https://sites.research.google/gr/neural-mapping/)
- LICONN (Google + ISTA, Nature 2025 "Light-microscopy-based connectomic reconstruction of mammalian brain tissue"): expansion-hydrogel light microscopy validated at scale with automated reconstruction of nearly 1 million µm^3 of mouse cortex — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/liconn-connectomics/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08985-1)
- SegCLR (2022): self-supervised embeddings identifying cell types and neuron parts from small fragments; ~8 billion embeddings released for H01 and MICrONS — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/multi-layered-mapping-of-brain-tissue-via-segmentation-guided-contrastive-learning/)
- TensorStore: C++/Python library for zarr, N5 and Neuroglancer-precomputed arrays; active releases v0.1.79 (Nov 11, 2025), v0.1.80 (Dec 10, 2025), v0.1.81 (Feb 6, 2026), v0.1.82 (Mar 13, 2026) — [GitHub releases](https://github.com/google/tensorstore/releases); [TensorStore docs](https://google.github.io/tensorstore/)
- Neuroglancer: WebGL viewer for petascale volumetric data, meshes and skeletons, with Python bindings; repository active through 2025–2026 (PRs, discussions dated to June 2026) but no formal versioned release notes found — [GitHub google/neuroglancer](https://github.com/google/neuroglancer); [Python README](https://github.com/google/neuroglancer/blob/master/python/README.md)

### Inferences
- The FlyWire segmentation itself (the base FAFB-FFN segmentation of 2019) originated from Google; Princeton/Seung lab layered CAVE/PyChunkedGraph proofreading and community annotation on top. This is consistent with Codex crediting "AI" for segments and Google's 2019 blog for the FAFB reconstruction, but I did not find an explicit statement that FlyWire's production segmentation is Google's FFN output rather than a Seung-lab re-segmentation (see gaps).
- Google's 2025–2026 connectomics focus has shifted to zebrafish (Fish1/ZAPBench), mouse hippocampus, LICONN and human tissue; the Sept 2026 MaleCNS blog is the main fly-related Google output of 2026.

### Gaps
- No source found explicitly stating which segmentation (Google FFN vs Seung-lab/Zetta) underlies the FlyWire production chunked graph; the Dorkenwald 2024 methods section (not fetched) would settle this.
- No formal Neuroglancer release notes for 2025–2026 located.

## Key Question 3: How to download and load the data programmatically (Python libraries, file sizes, formats, licensing)?

### Takeaway
FlyWire v783 is fully downloadable as Feather/CSV/Parquet from Zenodo (10.6 GB, CC BY 4.0) and Codex (CC BY-NC 4.0 per search summary); Python access is via fafbseg-py/navis (static exports and CAVE), R via fafbseg/natverse; Janelia datasets (hemibrain, optic-lobe:v1.1, male-cns:v1.0) are served by neuPrint (neuprint-python) plus flat-file GCS downloads under CC-BY; BANC has `banc` (PyPI) / `bancr` clients, Codex, and Harvard Dataverse.

- For an offline simulation pipeline the minimal FlyWire bundle is: proofread_connections_783.feather (852 MB; already aggregated pre→post edge weights with NT) + the flywire_annotations CSV (cell types, side, class) + Codex neuron/NT tables. The 9.5 GB per-synapse file is only needed for spatial/compartment-level modeling.
- The CC BY-NC restriction on Codex downloads matters only if the project is commercial; using the Zenodo CC BY 4.0 mirror (and Janelia CC-BY datasets) avoids the issue.

## Key Question 4: Are there existing whole-brain simulations built on FlyWire, and what 2025–2026 follow-ups exist?

### Takeaway
Yes: Shiu et al. (Nature, Oct 2024) built a Brian2 leaky integrate-and-fire model of all 127,400 proofread neurons in FlyWire v630 with one free parameter, and it has spawned 2025–2026 follow-ups including a 12-chip Intel Loihi 2 neuromorphic port (Aug 2025), the Eon Systems embodied fly in NeuroMechFly v2/MuJoCo (Mar 2026), a graph-RL "Fly-connectomic Graph Model" for locomotion (Feb–Jun 2026), and the optic-lobe-focused flyvis deep mechanistic network (Nature 2024).

### Cited Findings
- Shiu et al., "A Drosophila computational brain model reveals sensorimotor processing", Nature (Oct 2024): LIF model in Brian2 of "all 127,400 proofread neurons from Flywire materialization v.630", ~50 million synaptic connections; resting potential −52 mV, threshold −45 mV, membrane time constant ~11 ms, synaptic decay τ 5 ms, refractory 2.2 ms, single free parameter Wsyn = 0.275 mV per synapse; NT sign from ML predictions (excitatory ACh, inhibitory GABA/Glu); 91% accuracy across 164 experimentally testable predictions; omits gap junctions, neuropeptides, neuromodulation, assumes zero baseline firing — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446845/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9); code — [GitHub philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model](https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model); [Berkeley News](https://news.berkeley.edu/2024/10/02/researchers-simulate-an-entire-fly-brain-on-a-laptop-is-a-human-brain-next/); earlier bioRxiv version (May 2023) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37205514/)
- Wang, Theilman, Rothganger, Severa, Vineyard, Aimone (Sandia), "Neuromorphic Simulation of Drosophila Melanogaster Brain Connectome on Loihi 2", arXiv Aug 22, 2025: FlyWire connectome (140,000 neurons, 50 M synapses) on 12 Loihi 2 chips; "orders of magnitude faster" than conventional hardware, advantage growing with sparser activity; handled fan-in/fan-out memory limits — [arXiv 2508.16792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16792)
- Eon Systems (Mar 10, 2026), "How the Eon Team Produced a Virtual Embodied Fly": LIF model based on FlyWire (Dorkenwald 2024, ~140k neurons / ~50 M synapses) coupled to NeuroMechFly v2 on MuJoCo; closed sensorimotor loop for taste-guided navigation to food, grooming when "dust" accumulates, and feeding; team includes Philip Shiu, led by Alex Wissner-Gross; code availability not stated — [Eon](https://eon.systems/updates/embodied-brain-emulation)
- Jin, Zhu, Zhang, Sui, "Whole-Brain Connectomic Graph Model Enables Whole-Body Locomotion Control in Fruit Fly", arXiv Feb 20, 2026 (revised June 14, 2026): instantiates the whole-brain connectome as a graph-structured neural controller for a simulated biomechanical fly trained with deep RL; better sample efficiency than graph/non-graph baselines; dataset and simulator not identified in the abstract — [arXiv 2602.17997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17997)
- Lappalainen et al., "Connectome-constrained networks predict neural activity across the fly visual system", Nature 2024: deep mechanistic network with measured connectivity and task-optimized unknown parameters, ~45,000 neurons across 721 columns of the central visual field, predicts ON/OFF split and T4/T5 direction selectivity; code flyvis (PyTorch, MIT, on PyPI) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07939-3); [GitHub TuragaLab/flyvis](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/flyvis/)
- "State of Brain Emulation Report 2025" (arXiv Oct 2025; Philip Shiu among authors) covers neural dynamics, connectomics and emulation/embodiment — [arXiv 2510.15745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15745)
- Hobbyist/derivative projects driven by live LIF simulation of FlyWire subsets (e.g., a 668-neuron circuit with 18,968 connection rows including LC4/LPLC2 looming detectors, 1 kHz) exist (DesktopFly, Aug 2026) — [GitHub desktop-fly](https://github.com/robmul165/desktop-fly); curated list — [awesome-fly](https://github.com/cobanov/awesome-fly)
- Emulation frameworks comparing Brian2, Brian2CUDA, PyTorch, NEST GPU and neuromorphic chips for the fly brain — [GitHub eonsystemspbc/fly-brain](https://github.com/eonsystemspbc/fly-brain)
- Related analyses: "Visual Function Profiles via Multi-Path Aggregation Reveal Neuron-Level Responses in the Drosophila Brain" (arXiv Dec 2025) — [arXiv 2512.06934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06934); "Network geometry of the Drosophila brain" (arXiv Feb 2026) — [arXiv 2602.16417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16417)

### Inferences
- The Shiu model's parameters (LIF, Wsyn 0.275 mV, τ 5 ms, ~11 ms membrane) are the de-facto baseline for whole-brain FlyWire spiking simulations and are directly reusable; its known omissions (gap junctions, neuromodulation, spontaneous activity, and — critically for vision — graded-potential photoreceptor/lamina neurons) are the areas where a visual-system model must diverge.
- No published whole-CNS (brain + VNC) spiking simulation on BANC or MaleCNS was found as of Sept 2026; embodied efforts (Eon, Jin et al.) still use the brain-only FlyWire graph with hand-crafted or RL-learned motor interfaces.

### Gaps
- Whether Shiu et al. or others have released an updated model on v783 or on the July-2025 synapse tables was not found.
- Code availability for the Loihi 2 and Jin et al. works not confirmed.

## Key Question 5: Which tools/datasets are practically reusable for a connectome-constrained simulation of the fly visual system (NT predictions, optic-lobe cell types, eye maps, hexagonal column IDs)?

### Takeaway
Reusable building blocks exist in every layer: per-synapse NT probabilities (Eckstein/synister; in Zenodo v783 and Codex), consolidated optic-lobe cell types (Matsliah 2024 in Codex; Nern 2025 optic-lobe:v1.1 with 732 types), hexagonal column/ommatidia assignments (Codex "Visual Columns Map"/"Visual Field Map" for FAFB; Berg 2025 L1/R7/R8 column table for MaleCNS; Mi1-M10-based medulla grid in Zhao et al. 2025), and a ready-made column-tiled model (flyvis, 721 columns) to bootstrap the optic lobe.

### Cited Findings
- Codex provides, for FAFB optic-lobe data, a Visual Cell Types Catalog, a Visual Field Map and a Visual Columns Map, and displays predicted NT type + confidence and verified NT/neuropeptide annotations (which may disagree) — [Codex FAQ](https://codex.flywire.ai/faq)
- Matsliah et al. 2024: Codex maps cell types to hexagonal column and ommatidia locations for spatial connectivity analyses; ~800 ommatidia per eye map to lamina columns in a hexagonal lattice — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07981-1)
- Zhao et al. (Nature 2025, "Eye structure shapes neuron function in Drosophila motion vision"): many ommatidial unit hexagons are irregular (inter-ommatidial and shear angles); medulla columns are identified by the center of mass of Mi1 M10 arbors and mapped onto a 2D regular grid with +h/+v axes defined by the equator and central meridian — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09276-5); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.12.14.520178v1.full)
- Berg et al. 2025 MaleCNS supplement includes optic lobe column assignments (L1, R7, R8 with column type classification, Excel) — [GitHub 2025malecns](https://github.com/flyconnectome/2025malecns)
- Nern et al. 2025 / optic-lobe:v1.1: 732 types, ~53,000 neurons, cell-type explorer, neuPrint, CC-BY — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08746-0); [Janelia](https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/optic-lobe)
- flyvis: ~45,000 neurons over 721 columns with cell-type-level connectivity; PyTorch, MIT license; supports pretrained models and custom models — [GitHub](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07939-3)
- Per-synapse NT probabilities for 6 transmitters are in flywire_synapses_783.feather (Zenodo) — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10676866); per-neuron NT and hierarchical annotations in flywire_annotations — [GitHub](https://github.com/flyconnectome/flywire_annotations)
- Unicolumnar FlyWire types with ~790 instances per hemisphere: L1–L5, Mi1, Mi4, Mi9, Tm1, Tm2, Tm9, Tm20, Dm2, Dm8, T1, T4a–d, T5a–d, C2, C3, R7, R8 — [FlyWire blog](https://blog.flywire.ai/2024/12/20/optic-lobe-tag-rally/)
- Heterogeneity of synaptic connectivity across columns in the fly visual system (Nat Commun 2024) — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-45971-z)
- Neuroglancer (Google) is the shared viewer across FlyWire, BANC (ng.banc.community), Janelia neuPrint datasets and MaleCNS — [GitHub google/neuroglancer](https://github.com/google/neuroglancer); [BANC-project](https://github.com/htem/BANC-project)
- Cross-dataset cell-type alignment tables: FlyWire↔hemibrain (Schlegel 2024) and MaleCNS↔FlyWire aligned edges (Berg 2025) — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07686-5); [GitHub](https://github.com/flyconnectome/2025malecns)

### Inferences
- A practical stack: (1) flyvis or the Codex Visual Columns Map to define the hexagonal column lattice and eye map; (2) FlyWire v783 proofread connections + NT probabilities for the whole brain (Shiu-style LIF); (3) optic-lobe:v1.1 / MaleCNS for a fully proofread, column-annotated visual system and for adding VNC/motor output; (4) Neuroglancer/navis for inspection.
- Because Nern 2025 and Matsliah 2024 both name optic-lobe types (with roughly half of Nern's types newly named), type-name harmonization between FAFB and male datasets will be needed; the flywire_annotations repo already incorporates optic-lobe annotations and MaleCNS cross-validation, which is the natural place to get a harmonized table.

### Gaps
- The exact download endpoint/format of Codex's column-ID tables for FAFB (per-neuron hex coordinates) was not retrieved (login required).
- No source found describing an existing simulation that combines BANC or MaleCNS optic lobes with a VNC motor model.
