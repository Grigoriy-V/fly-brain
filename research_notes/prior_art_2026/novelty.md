

## verdict

No published paper, preprint, or released project reconstructs the visual stimulus itself per cell type or per processing stage from a connectome-constrained fly visual model — every fly result I could retrieve decodes a low-dimensional label (optic flow, motion direction, orientation, characters, actions), so the per-stage luminance-decodability map is not taken, but three of the four things called novel are weaker than stated.

## what_is_novel

Novel, as far as retrieval can establish: a per-cell-type map of how much of the rendered luminance image is linearly recoverable from a connectome-constrained fly visual model, reported as a decodability ordering across the hierarchy against a shuffle floor - no fly source decodes the stimulus itself rather than a label. Taking the four claims separately: (a) decoding an untrained variable from a frozen connectome-constrained fly model is ALREADY TAKEN in the peer-reviewed literature (Dhiman & Panwar, Neuroinformatics 2026, motion direction across scales) - what is unclaimed is the target, full-field 721-hexal luminance, and I could not verify the pre-2000 blowfly photoreceptor/LMC literature, where Wiener-filter reconstruction of naturalistic intensity time courses from real recordings may constitute earlier prior art in spirit; (b) the per-cell-type ordering across the hierarchy is UNCLAIMED IN THE FLY but is a standard mammalian analysis (Chen et al., PLOS CB 2024, mouse brain hierarchy), so it is a transfer, not a new question, and the direction of the result (T4/T5 lowest) is what the biology predicts; (c) is NOT YET TRUE OF THIS RESULT - the measurement ran on pretrained flyvis, which the Nature paper states is spatially homogeneous and hexagonally convolutional over averaged FIB-25/FIB-19 counts, and connectome-constrained modelling on individual complete connectomes is already common, so (c) buys nothing unless the reconstruction is actually run on MaleCNS; (d) is the weakest - both shuffle types are already used together on a connectome-constrained fly model (Dhiman & Panwar, Sci Rep 2026, label and connectivity shuffles), so only the specific scene-level/frame-specific decomposition is unclaimed, and I could not verify it is unprecedented in the wider decoding literature. The defensible claim is the conjunction: stimulus-level reconstruction, resolved per cell type, across the whole hierarchy of a connectome-constrained fly model, with controls.

## findings

- Nothing found, anywhere, that reconstructs the visual stimulus (image or video) from a connectome-constrained fly model and reports it per cell type or per stage with controls. Searched: OpenAlex (all 128 works citing Lappalainen et al. 2024, titles scanned), Europe PMC (full-text 'flyvis' returns exactly 4 records: Lappalainen 2024, Dhiman & Panwar 2026, Ozdil 2026, Zhou & Hasler 2026), arXiv API (connectome-constrained + Drosophila/decoding/reconstruction listings), OpenReview (NeurIPS/ICLR/CCN/MIDL), NeurIPS virtual site, GitHub code + repo search, and two curated fly-connectome lists.
- Verified directly in the Lappalainen et al. 2024 full text (PMC11525180), not from memory: the only decoder is a single two-layer convolutional optic-flow readout taking the pooled activity of medulla-and-downstream output cell types, trained end-to-end with the network; there is no per-cell-type decoding ranking, no luminance/image reconstruction anywhere, and the word 'shuffl' does not appear in the article at all. Their baseline is 50 DMNs with random Gaussian parameters whose decoder alone is task-optimized (Extended Data Fig. 2a) - a random-parameter control, not a shuffled-connectivity control.
- Also verified in that full text: flyvis is NOT an individual connectome. It rests on 'our assumption of spatial homogeneity (that is, the hexagonally convolutional structure of the network)', built from average synapse counts per (cell-type, column-offset) pair over 1,801 neurons from FIB-25 and FIB-19, tiled to 721 columns, leaving 734 free parameters. The pretrained member measured today therefore carries an averaged, idealised connectome - claim (c) is not a property of the current result.
- The same source shows the luminance target is close to an identity at the top of the hierarchy: 'The transduced luminance at each photoreceptor is the greyscale mean value in the 13 x 13-pixel region surrounding it.' PixCorr 1.000 for photoreceptors is therefore near-tautological (the decoder inverts the rendering, not a computation), so the result is the shape of the decline, not the ceiling.
- Closest published fly work is Dhiman & Panwar, Neuroinformatics 2026-08-21: a frozen pretrained FlyVis checkpoint with a linear decoder recovering MOTION DIRECTION across apparent scales (0.885 accuracy; T4/T5 silencing drops the original decoder to 0.193 while a refitted decoder reaches 0.891; parameter shuffles used as control). That establishes the frozen-flyvis-plus-linear-probe-for-an-untrained-variable move in the peer-reviewed literature. It contains no stimulus reconstruction. Caveat: Springer full text was paywalled, so these details come from the publisher landing page and a secondary summary, not the PDF.
- The same authors' Scientific Reports paper (2026-05-19, optic-lobe connectome v1.1 rate model) already pairs four-way motion decoding with BOTH label shuffling and connectivity shuffling, reporting that each collapses the decoder-based mutual-information bound to zero. So the two-shuffle control idiom on a connectome-constrained fly model is published; what is unpublished is using two shuffle types to decompose a reconstruction into scene-level and frame-specific parts.
- Zhou & Hasler (bioRxiv 2026-06-12, preprint) run RSA/CKA on the flyvis ensemble against randomised wiring and find real wiring gives 'a smooth circular direction geometry that random networks capture only coarsely' (r = 0.686-0.846), while conceding trained random networks look comparable. This is the nearest thing to a per-representation fidelity comparison on flyvis; it is geometry, not reconstruction, and not per cell type.
- The flyvis authors have no decoding follow-up that I can find. TuragaLab's repo list shows no decoder project; flyvis v1.2.0 (2026-08-06) ships only precomputed response-normalisation constants; and their 2026 flysim_tutorials 'project ideas' are stimulus DESIGN (MEI-style optimal and illusion stimuli), mechanism discovery, cross-cell-type clustering, and auxiliary training targets - the inverse direction (activity -> stimulus) is not among them.
- Hobby/GitHub layer is broad but shallow on this axis. Both curated lists (townie/awesome-fruit-fly, cobanov/awesome-fly) contain only classification or RL readouts from frozen connectomes: fly_ocr (266k-param decoder, 87.6% on 1,632 glyphs), swat-or-buy, FLM (278k-param adapter into a 1.2B LM), Fly Dino, Fly Tic-Tac-Toe, Scrollfly, FLYNN, fly-fpv. None reconstructs an image, none reports decodability per cell type or per stage.
- The single closest hobby artifact is zachvanl/fly-eye-artist: a frozen flyvis optic lobe rendering a picture into 721 hexagonal facets, whose README pre-empts exactly this comparison - 'There is no CNN/ViT/diffusion frontend and no decoder trained to reconstruct pictures.' Its 'Gallery A greyscale BoxEye reconstruction' is the forward hexagonal sampling of the input, not a decode of activity.
- Two other projects are nearer than they look and neither takes the ground: Aaryan658/connectome-ground-truth runs logistic/ridge probes on flyvis representations for stimulus direction, ON/OFF polarity and speed (labels, not pixels; per interpretability method, not per cell type); NSSIL/ScaleBreak-FlyVis is a reproduction toolkit for the Dhiman & Panwar linear-decodability-across-scale result. Sudharsanselvaraj/The-Hawking-Fly couples flyvis to MaleCNS but writes in its own code that 'Phase 0A validates sanity only; nothing here is a decodability claim.'
- The per-stage decodability ordering is an established analysis in mammals, so (b) is novel only as a transfer: Chen et al., PLOS CB 2024 decode dynamic visual scenes across the mouse brain hierarchy and tie decoding performance to published hierarchy indices. Encoder-inversion reconstruction is likewise established outside the fly (Bauer et al., eLife reviewed preprint 2026, mouse V1 movies; Brackbill et al., eLife 2020, linear reconstruction from primate RGCs at rho 0.76).
- Building a connectome-constrained model on an individual complete connectome is already crowded, so (c) carries no novelty on its own: Liew et al. (NeurIPS 2025 poster, arXiv:2609.01330) run a complete fly brain connectome as LIF spiking neurons and extract orientation maps; Li et al. (bioRxiv 2026-08-25) fit a FlyWire whole-brain connectome-constrained model to spontaneous calcium activity; dozens of MaleCNS hobby simulators do the same. What no one has done on such a connectome is per-stage stimulus reconstruction.
- Adjacent-but-not-competing work found while searching, worth knowing: Xie et al. (arXiv:2512.06934) predict neuron-level responses from whole-brain connectome topology (encoding, the opposite direction); Li et al. 2026 is the closest published anchor for the project's spontaneous-activity arm; Brunton lab's 'digital sphinx' (bioRxiv 2026-03-20) is the standing warning that a trained readout on a frozen connectome can look convincing while carrying no biological content - it applies directly to a ridge decoder on frozen flyvis.
- Gaps in the reported measurement that a reviewer will reach for before novelty: one flyvis member rather than the 50-model ensemble spread, no shuffled-connectivity control, no random-parameter control of the kind Lappalainen et al. themselves used, and only the sample-shuffle floor (0.117) - the time-shuffle half of the claimed two-control decomposition is not in the numbers given.

## applied_recommendation

Claim novelty only for the conjunction - per-cell-type luminance reconstruction across the fly hierarchy with controls - and in the same sentence cite Dhiman & Panwar (Neuroinformatics 2026) for the frozen-flyvis linear-probe move and Chen et al. (PLOS CB 2024) for the per-stage decodability ordering; drop (c) until the decoder actually runs on MaleCNS, since the measured number came from flyvis's averaged hexagonally-convolutional connectome; stop reporting photoreceptor PixCorr 1.000 as a result, because flyvis defines photoreceptor activity as the greyscale mean of the 13x13-pixel patch; and before any of this goes into a report, add the 50-model ensemble spread, a shuffled-connectivity control and the missing time-shuffle arm.

## could_not_verify

- Springer full text of Dhiman & Panwar, Neuroinformatics 2026 (10.1007/s12021-026-09811-3) - the URL redirects to an IdP authorization endpoint. Its decoder details, exact shuffle controls and whether any supplementary analysis touches stimulus reconstruction come from the publisher landing page and the bioengineer.org summary, not the PDF.
- bioRxiv's own search and article pages returned HTTP 403/429 throughout; bioRxiv coverage here is indirect, via OpenAlex and Europe PMC, whose full-text indexing of preprints is incomplete. A bioRxiv preprint posted in the last weeks could be missed.
- Semantic Scholar's API returned HTTP 429 on every attempt, so no citation-graph or embedding-based neighbour search was run there.
- Classic real-recording fly literature on reconstructing naturalistic light-intensity time courses from photoreceptor or large-monopolar-cell responses (de Ruyter van Steveninck / Laughlin / Juusola lineage). Searches returned information-rate, SNR and contrast-gain papers but no reconstruction paper I could cite; I cannot rule out that 'reconstruct luminance from early fly visual neurons' exists there for real neurons, which would weaken the framing of (a) further.
- Which connectome Liew et al. (arXiv:2609.01330 / NeurIPS 2025) actually used, and what controls they ran - the abstract says only 'a complete fruit fly brain connectome', and neither the arXiv abstract page nor the project README specifies FlyWire vs MaleCNS or lists controls.
- Whether the Turaga lab has unpublished or in-review decoding work. Only public artefacts (repos, releases, tutorials, indexed publications) were checked; absence there is not absence in the lab.
- GitHub code search only indexes default branches of public repos it has crawled, and the hobby-project layer churns fast; a private or very recent reconstruction demo would not appear.
- Non-English venues, closed workshops, and conference abstracts not indexed by OpenAlex, Europe PMC, arXiv or OpenReview.
