

## verdict

Fly colour biology is well quantified and connectome-constrained colour circuit models already exist (Heath 2020, Christenson 2024), so spectral R7/R8 channels are not a new idea; what is unoccupied is doing it at 721-column retinotopic scale with a pale/yellow mosaic and measuring per-cell-type chromatic decodability with controls — but not on flyvis, whose connectome contains no Dm8 and no Dm9 (verified locally), which is why the cheapest honest version runs on MaleCNS where R7p/R7y/R8p/R8y, Dm8a/Dm8b and Dm9 all exist.

## what_is_novel

Not novel: spectrally distinct R7/R8 channels driving a connectome-wired circuit (Heath et al. 2020; Christenson et al. 2024 - the latter explicitly connectomics-constrained, with pDm8/yDm8, Tm5a/b/c, Tm20, and the finding that recurrence generates hue selectivity), the measurement of R7/R8 opponency, and the claim that four opsin channels compress onto two opponent axes (Heath et al. 2020, 97% of chromatic variance). A connectome-wide network trained on a colour-discrimination task also already exists in unpublished form (Correig-Fraga et al., FlyWire v783, code plus Zenodo data, no preprint found). Novel, as far as retrieved sources show: (1) a retinotopic, 721-column-scale connectome-constrained model in which the pale/yellow ommatidial mosaic is instantiated per column from connectome annotations rather than assumed, driven by spatially structured spectral stimuli rather than full-field LEDs; (2) per-cell-type chromatic decodability curves - how much of the chromatic stimulus is linearly recoverable from each cell type's population activity - reported against sample-shuffle, shuffled-connectivity and equiluminance controls, which no retrieved paper does in any species-specific connectome model; (3) the comparison of chromatic versus luminance decodability at the same stages, which is the actual new axis relative to this project's existing luminance result. The mechanistic claim 'recurrence builds hue selectivity' is already taken; the informational claim 'here is where chromatic information is kept or discarded, stage by stage, with controls' is not.

## findings

- [Biology - opsins] Pale ommatidia: R7 expresses Rh3, R8 expresses Rh5; yellow: R7 Rh4, R8 Rh6; R1-6 express Rh1. lambda-max values differ between in-vitro/ectopic and in-vivo measurement, and the literature circulates two sets attributed to Salcedo et al. 1999: sensitivity maxima 478 (Rh1), 420 (Rh2), 345 (Rh3), 375 (Rh4), 437 (Rh5), 508 nm (Rh6), versus absorbance maxima quoted as 331/355 (Rh3/Rh4) and 442/515 (Rh5/Rh6). Any model must name which set it used (Salcedo et al. 1999, J Neurosci 19:10716).
- [Biology - opsins in vivo] Sharkey et al. 2020 (Sci Rep, 10.1038/s41598-020-74742-1) measured in vivo in red-eyed flies: R1-6 490 nm, R7p 330, R7y 355, R8p 435, R8y 600 nm. The 600 nm R8y figure is explicitly attributed by the authors to long-wavelength leakage through screening pigment plus the blue-absorbing yellow filter, not to Rh6 itself; R1-6 show the triple-peaked UV signature of the UV-sensitising pigment on top of a 490 nm visual pigment. So the in-vivo R8y number is not a usable Rh6 peak.
- [Biology - modelling curves used before] Heath et al. 2020 built their circuit model with peaks 360 (Rh3), 380 (Rh4), 420 (Rh5), 500 nm (Rh6) - a third, different set from the same literature. Christenson et al. 2024 used curves fit to published spectral data and six LEDs at 340, 360, 415, 455, 565, 615 nm.
- [Biology - mosaic] Pale:yellow is stochastic, set by spineless: ~30:70 in the classic account (Wernet et al. 2006, Nature), 35:65 as used by Heath et al. 2020, and 297/779 = 38% pale measured in the FAFB medulla by Kind et al. 2021. Specialisations: dorsal rim area ommatidia express Rh3 in BOTH R7 and R8 for polarised skylight (42 DRA columns identified in FAFB); in the dorsal third, Iro-C drives Rh3+Rh4 co-expression in yR7 while R8 keeps the blue/green split (Mazzoni et al. 2008, PLoS Biol 6:e97).
- [Biology - opponency mechanism] Colour opponency appears already in R7/R8 axon terminals, built by two parallel histaminergic mechanisms: direct axo-axonal reciprocal inhibition between R7 and R8 of the same ommatidium via HisCl1, and feedback from the multicolumnar glutamatergic Dm9 via Ort (Schnaitmann et al. 2018, Cell 172:318-330; Schnaitmann et al. 2024, Front Mol Neurosci). Dm9 feedback is sufficient for opponency in R7 but not in R8, which additionally needs HisCl1 inhibition from its partner R7.
- [Biology - Dm9 geometry and coding] Dm9 arborises in M1-M6 across ~7 neighbouring columns; Heath et al. 2020's anatomically constrained linear recurrent model of R7p/R7y/R8p/R8y + Dm9 predicts a UV-versus-visible opponent centre of one ommatidium (~5 deg) with an antagonistic achromatic surround (~35 deg), and their PCA shows the four opsin channels compress onto two opponent axes retaining 97% of chromatic variance. Measured signs: pR7/yR7 excited 320-440 nm, inhibited longer; pR8 tri-lobed (excited 400-500, inhibited by UV and green); yR8 excited 400-620, inhibited by UV.
- [Biology - downstream targets, FAFB] Kind et al. 2021 (eLife 10:e71858) quantified R7/R8 output: Dm9 16.4% of synapses (64% from R8, 36% from R7), Dm8 14.6% (R7-exclusive, no R8 input), MeTu 6.5% (R7-only), Tm5c 5.2% (87.9% R8), Tm20 5.2% (94.3% R8), Mi15 4.9% (90.2% R8), Dm2 3.5% (81.2% pale-biased), Tm5a 2.0% (yellow-exclusive). Tm5a is selective for yellow R7, Tm5b for pale R7. Each R7 synapses onto ~8 Dm8 on average (15 contacted).
- [Biology - Dm8 function] Each Dm8 pools ~16 R7 inputs and relays to Tm5c; R7 to Dm8 to Tm5c is required for UV preference (Karuppudurai et al. 2014, Neuron 81:603-615). Dm8 also shows spatio-chromatic (double) opponency - UV/visible opponency built from direct R7 input against indirect broadband R1-6 input via Mi1/Tm3 (Li et al. 2021, Curr Biol 31:3040-3052), and is excited by long-wavelength light through Mi1 from R1-6 (Pagni et al. 2021, Curr Biol). So the chromatic pathway is not separable from the R1-6 achromatic pathway.
- [Biology - hue selectivity] Tm5a, Tm5b and Tm20 are nonlinearly hue-selective (selectivity index kappa about 3), while Tm5c is broadband and approximately linear (Christenson et al. 2024, Nat Neurosci, peer reviewed, 16 May 2024). These are exactly the cell types a per-stage decodability study would target, and their measured tunings are an available validation target.
- [Biology - behaviour] Tm5a, Tm5b, Tm5c and Tm20 are redundantly required for intensity-independent hue discrimination (Melnattur et al. 2014, J Neurogenet 28:374-388), and broadband R1-6 also contribute to colour discrimination behaviourally (Schnaitmann et al. 2013, Curr Biol) - so a model with only R7/R8 spectral channels and no R1-6 spectral treatment is already known to be incomplete.
- [Modelling - what exists 1] A connectome-constrained colour model already exists twice over: Heath et al. 2020 (linear recurrent, EM synapse counts as weights, R7p/R7y/R8p/R8y + Dm9) and Christenson et al. 2024 (FAFB/FlyWire-constrained recurrent model with linear synaptic integration plus tanh, including R7/R8, pDm8/yDm8, Tm5a, Tm5b, Tm5c, Tm20, Mi3, Sm31, Sm40; recurrence shown to be necessary for hue selectivity, confirmed by genetic silencing).
- [Modelling - the gap in those models] Christenson et al. state that all their tunings were measured with full-field stimulus presentations, i.e. no spatial structure, no ommatidial mosaic sampling of an image, no natural reflectances; and they performed no decoding, information-theoretic or dimensionality analysis of hue from the population. So per-stage chromatic decodability over a retinotopic image is not what these papers did.
- [Modelling - flyvis has no colour, verified in source] flyvis 1.2.0 as installed in this project contains zero occurrences of 'wavelength', 'opsin', 'rhodopsin', 'chromatic' or 'spectral' (grep over D:/ML/Fly_Brain/.venv/Lib/site-packages/flyvis). The Nature methods confirm the input: RGB frames are transformed to normalised greyscale, 64/65 cell types, 721 columns, optic-flow task. The published ensemble has 50 members (50 member directories under data/flyvis/results/flow/0000).
- [Modelling - decisive flyvis limitation] The flyvis connectome (fib25-fib19_v2.2.json, extent 15 -> 721 columns) has 65 node types with R1-R8 as the 8 input units, and contains NO Dm cell types at all - no Dm8, no Dm9. The chromatic projection neurons are present (R7 -> Tm5b 12.6 and Tm5a 12.6 mean synapses; R8 -> Tm20 12.8, Mi15 12.5, Tm5c 8.3, Mi4 16.3, Mi1 11.4), and the intra-ommatidial reciprocal pair exists with fixed inhibitory sign (R8 -> R7 18.3, R7 -> R8 1.3, both alpha = -1, i.e. the HisCl1 mechanism). But the Dm9 feedback loop and the Dm8 pooling stage - the two elements that generate opponency and hue selectivity in the published models - are structurally absent. Also there is a single R7 and a single R8 per column, so pale/yellow must be imposed as a per-column label, not as separate cell types.
- [Modelling - no flyvis colour extension] The flyvis repository has two open issues (licensing of pretrained models, a solver.recover signature bug) and nothing about colour, chromatic input, wavelength, opsins or multi-channel photoreceptor input (checked 18 Sep 2026).
- [Modelling - nearest existing 'colour + connectome + trained network'] Correig-Fraga, Guimera and Sales-Pardo, 'Structure alone supports efficient visual computation in the Drosophila visual system': FlyWire v783 whole-brain graph with fixed structure, learning only a scalar gain per synapse plus a linear readout, an anatomically faithful compound-eye model (Voronoi catchment per ommatidium seeded at R7 positions) where each photoreceptor type reads the image channel matching its spectral sensitivity, trained on colour discrimination (yellow versus blue), shape and numerosity, and compared against randomised wirings. Verified: GitHub repo and Zenodo data record 10.5281/zenodo.21549559 (25 Jul 2026). Not verified: any preprint or journal version, and whether the spectral channels are real opsin curves or simply RGB channel assignment (the README wording 'reads the channel matching its spectral sensitivity' suggests the latter).
- [Modelling - whole-connectome work still lumps R7/R8] Hoeller, Zhao, Nern, Rogers, Romani and Reiser (Cell 189(18):5552-5570.e10, 3 Sep 2026; bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.22.696097) analyse the male CNS connectome with graph/information-flow methods only (no dynamics) and treat R7 and R8 as single input channels plus R7d/R8d for the DRA - they do not split pale and yellow. The FlyWire optic-lobe parts list (Matsliah, Nern et al., Nature 2024) says explicitly that the Dm8a/Dm8b to yDm8/pDm8 correspondence 'remains speculative' and that classifying inner photoreceptors as yellow and pale is postponed to a future release.
- [Feasibility on MaleCNS - annotations, local check] MaleCNS v1.0 annotations already downloaded by this project (data/malecns/body-annotations-male-cns-v1.0-minconf-0.5.feather) carry pale/yellow identity in the 'type' field: R7p 332, R7y 482, R7d 82, R7_unclear 404; R8p 330, R8y 481, R8d 76, R8_unclear 442, R7R8_unclear 85. Among classified cells p:y = 0.41:0.59 for both R7 and R8 - noticeably more pale than the 30:70 or 35:65 literature ratios, which matters because the unclassified ~400 per class could be biased. The chromatic interneurons are all present: Dm8a 572, Dm8b 532, Dm9 273, Tm5a 624, Tm5b 522, Tm5c 750, Tm20 1762, Mi15 1151, Dm-DRA1 50, Dm-DRA2 33.
- [Feasibility on MaleCNS - wiring check, local computation] Summing MaleCNS v1.0 weights (data/malecns/connectome-weights-male-cns-v1.0-minconf-0.5.feather, 176,632 edges out of the four inner photoreceptor classes) resolves the subtype question in the male dataset: R7p -> Dm8b 28.3% of its output versus Dm8a 6.1%, and R7y -> Dm8a 38.7%; R7y -> Tm5a 10.4% while R7p -> Tm5b 6.2%. That is, Dm8a = yDm8 and Dm8b = pDm8, and the Tm5a-yellow / Tm5b-pale selectivity of Kind et al. reproduces in MaleCNS. R8p -> Dm9 20.5% and -> R7p 15.8%; R8y -> Dm9 20.0%, -> R7y 15.7%, -> Mi15 12.7%, -> Tm5c 8.3% - matching the FAFB pattern (Dm9 the largest single target, ~two thirds from R8) and confirming the reciprocal intra-ommatidial pair.
- [Feasibility - per-column mosaic] Berg et al. supplemental file optic-column-type-assignments-v1.0.xlsx gives, for every visual column of both male optic lobes, the L1/R7/R8 body ids and the column type (pale, yellow, edge, dorsal rim area), with vertical aMe12 and Tm5a branches as the anatomical markers used to call pale versus yellow. This is a third-party inference to verify, not a fact: the marker rule is exactly what produces the 404 'unclear' R7s and the 41:59 ratio above.
- [Task-relevance for a trained model] Whether optic-flow training would preserve chromatic information is genuinely contested in the literature: Yamaguchi et al. 2008 (PNAS 105:4910) show the optomotor response vanishes at equiluminance and is absent in flies lacking R1-6 rhodopsin, i.e. motion vision is colour-blind; Wardill et al. 2012 (Science 336:925) show R7/R8 do feed the motion pathway and that multiple spectral inputs improve motion discrimination. This pair is the scientific justification for retraining with chromatic channels rather than only probing a luminance-trained net.
- [Stimulus problem] There is no UV in Sintel. Converting sRGB frames into four or five opsin activations invents the UV channel, so any colour number obtained that way is a property of the conversion assumption. Usable alternatives: synthetic narrowband/broadband spectra as in Christenson et al. (six LEDs, 340-615 nm) rendered on the hexagonal lattice, and real hyperspectral natural scenes from the open 350-950 nm scanner database (Sci Rep 10.1038/s41598-019-47220-6).
- [Prior art on 'colour information along a hierarchy'] I found no study, in fly or in any connectome-constrained model, that reports per-stage or per-cell-type chromatic decodability with controls. The closest information-theoretic treatment is Malo 2022 (Entropy 24:1442), which is human colour appearance models, not a connectome: it reports ~75% redundancy reduction, opponent channels contributing the largest information gain (~2.5-2.8 bits) and nonlinear saturation ~1.0 bit. Heath et al. 2020's 4-channels-to-2-axes/97%-variance result is the fly-specific precedent for the compression claim.
- [Adjacent 2026 connectome-model work, all luminance] arXiv:2609.01330 (1 Sep 2026, preprint) derives orientation maps from connectome-constrained spiking dynamics with no chromatic component reported; bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.02.02.700492 (brainwide visual processing) could not be retrieved. Nothing found that combines spectral channels, a retinotopic mosaic and stimulus decoding.

## applied_recommendation

Do not run this on the pretrained flyvis ensemble as the headline experiment: its connectome has no Dm8 and no Dm9 (verified), so a "chromatic information collapses after R7/R8" result would be a property of missing cells, and its parameters were fitted with identical luminance fed to all eight receptors, so differential spectral input is off-distribution. Cheapest version that yields a real result, in three steps with a decision gate after each: (Step 0, free, hours, local) build the input model only - five channels (Rh1 plus UV-sensitising pigment for R1-6, Rh3, Rh4, Rh5, Rh6, with the chosen lambda-max set named in config.toml alongside its source) and a per-column pale/yellow mosaic taken from the MaleCNS column-type assignments (and cross-checked against the R7p->Dm8b / R7y->Dm8a asymmetry measured here), then compute the input-side upper bound on chromatic decodability and the conditioning of the opsin mixing matrix. If the mixing is near-invertible and the network is close to linear, decodability restates matrix rank and the experiment is not worth running as framed - this is the cheap go/no-go. (Step 1, cheap, local or one short GPU job) probe the existing pretrained flyvis ensemble with the five-channel input as an explicitly labelled off-distribution control, decoding luminance, the two opponent axes and hue angle at equiluminance per cell type, with the same sample-shuffle floor (0.117 baseline already established), a shuffled-connectivity control and a metamer condition where equiluminant pairs make a luminance decoder useless. (Step 2, the actual result, needs a Modal gate) build the chromatic model on MaleCNS, where R7p/R7y/R8p/R8y, Dm8a/Dm8b and Dm9 all exist, and validate it before claiming anything: it must reproduce the published opponency signs in R7/R8 terminals (Heath 2020, Schnaitmann 2018) and the hue selectivity of Tm5a/Tm5b/Tm20 versus broadband Tm5c (Christenson 2024) - that validation is the equivalent of flyvis's DSI/FRI check and is what turns per-stage decodability into a result rather than an illustration. Use synthetic LED-like spectra (340-615 nm, as in Christenson) plus open hyperspectral natural scenes for stimuli; never derive UV from sRGB Sintel frames. Frame the training question with the Yamaguchi 2008 versus Wardill 2012 tension: if the task stays optic flow, state up front that the loss exerts no pressure to preserve hue, so "discarded" is a statement about the objective, not about the fly.

## could_not_verify

- Exact Salcedo et al. 1999 lambda-max table - I did not open the J Neurosci article itself; two different number sets (345/375/437/508 and 331/355/442/515 nm) are attributed to it in secondary sources, and Heath et al. 2020 model with a third set (360/380/420/500).
- Christenson et al. 2024 model scale - how many neurons and how many columns were actually simulated. The PMC text I retrieved does not state it and the bioRxiv PDF returned HTTP 429 on two attempts; the multi-column description came from a page summary, so treat 'spatially extended' as unconfirmed.
- Whether Correig-Fraga, Guimera and Sales-Pardo have any preprint or peer-reviewed version (only GitHub code and Zenodo data record 10.5281/zenodo.21549559 verified), and how their photoreceptor spectral channels are actually implemented - the README says each type 'reads the channel matching its spectral sensitivity', which reads like RGB-channel assignment rather than opsin curves; the implementation lives in the separate cogstim package, which I did not inspect.
- Hoeller et al. Cell 2026 final text - cell.com returned 403, so my statements about their R7/R8 channel treatment come from the bioRxiv v1 full text; the published version may differ.
- bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.02.02.700492 'Connectome analysis reveals brainwide visual processing in Drosophila' - repeated HTTP 429, content unread; cannot rule out that it touches chromatic channels.
- Yamaguchi et al. PNAS 2010 'Contribution of photoreceptor subtypes to spectral wavelength preference' - pnas.org returned 403; I cite only its existence, no numbers from it.
- The two-axis categorical model formulas p = (E3-E5)/(E3+E5) and y = (E4-E6)/(E4+E6) appeared in a search-result summary with no attributable source; I could not pin them to a paper and did not use them.
- flyvis training cost - no GPU-hour or wall-time figure in the repository readme or the installed package, so I cannot price a retraining run; derive it from this project's own run log instead.
- Whether the MaleCNS R7_unclear/R8_unclear cells (404/442) are randomly distributed or biased toward one ommatidial type - this determines whether the 41:59 pale:yellow ratio I measured is real or an artefact of the aMe12/Tm5a marker rule used by Berg et al.
- Whether any private or very recent (post-search-index) work already adds spectral channels to flyvis - absence in search results is weak evidence; I verified only that the public repository has no such issue, PR or code.
