

## verdict

Substantially confirmed but misattributed and over-stated: an August 2026 bioRxiv preprint (Li, Ping, Zhang & Wang, 2026-08-25, DOI 10.64898/2026.08.21.745055) does fit a FlyWire v783 whole-brain rate model (138,639 neurons, 15,091,983 synapses) to resting-state Drosophila calcium recordings with topology and E/I sign frozen and per-synapse weight magnitudes plus per-neuron time constants learned — but the fitting target is 73 neuropil-averaged ΔF/F traces at 1.2 Hz from a single fly (not neuron-resolved activity), it is from a Chinese group (GDIIST / Beijing Normal), not Turaga/Murthy/Seung/Janelia/the FlyWire consortium, and neither code nor trained weights are released yet.

## findings

- THE PAPER EXISTS. Exact title: 'Connectome-constrained modeling identifies neurons and synapses that sustain spontaneous activity in Drosophila'. Authors: Qianzhu Li (1,2), Wenhao Ping (1,2) [equal contribution], Ke Zhang (2,3,4), Chaoming Wang (1, corresponding) - (1) Guangdong Institute of Intelligence Science and Technology, Hengqin, (2) Dept. of Systems Science, Beijing Normal University Zhuhai, (3) International Academic Center of Complex Systems BNU, (4) School of Systems Science BNU. bioRxiv v1, submitted 2026-08-21, posted 2026-08-25, DOI 10.64898/2026.08.21.745055, licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, bioRxiv 'published' field = NA (no journal version at retrieval). It is a PREPRINT, not peer-reviewed.
- THE CLAIM'S ATTRIBUTION IS WRONG. None of Turaga, Murthy, Seung, Ahrens, Pachitariu, Deisseroth, the FlyWire consortium or Janelia are authors. (Janelia appears only on the companion methods paper: Xiao Liu is a co-author of the BrainTrace Nature Communications paper.) Lappalainen et al. 2024 and Shiu et al. 2024 appear in the preprint only as references 45 and 44.
- WHICH CONNECTOME AND HOW BIG: FlyWire v783 (the female adult brain), after 'standard quality filtering' 138,639 neurons and 15,091,983 chemical synapses. Each model neuron maps one-to-one onto a FlyWire neuron and inherits its partners, synapse counts and neurotransmitter-predicted E/I polarity. Excitatory fraction approximately 0.6. Gap junctions, graded transmission, neuropeptides and neuromodulation are absent (the authors state neuromodulation is 'folded into the fixed effective weights').
- WHAT RECORDINGS: NOT neuron-resolved. Whole-brain resting-state two-photon calcium imaging of head-fixed adult Drosophila expressing pan-neuronal GCaMP6s, taken from Turner, Mann & Clandinin (Current Biology 2021) with Mann et al. 2017; volumes at 1.2 Hz, motion-corrected, registered to a standard template, then AVERAGED within each of 73 anatomically defined Ito-atlas neuropils to give 73 neuropil-level dF/F traces. Resting state = no locomotion or prominent grooming. Each fly contributes at least ~14 min. All main-text fits come from ONE example fly (the supplement states this explicitly); n = 5 means five independently trained model replicates, not five flies. No stimulus and no behaviour: spontaneous activity only.
- TARGET PREPROCESSING: the dF/F traces are deconvolved through an explicit calcium forward model (single-exponential GCaMP6s kernel, tau_Ca = 1.5 s; rise time unresolved at dt = 1/1.2 s) into a non-negative neuropil population firing rate, then rescaled to a mean rate of 1 Hz. The supplement notes the calcium kernel acts as an approximately 10-second low-pass filter on the training target and warns that the low-frequency dominance of the fitted dynamics is partly this confound.
- WHAT WAS FIXED: (a) support - a weight exists only on the ~1.5e7 connected ordered pairs out of ~1.9e10 possible, so anatomy pins >99.9% of the matrix to zero; (b) sign - sgn(j) in {+1,-1} frozen from FlyWire neurotransmitter predictions, all of a neuron's outgoing weights share one sign; (c) readout - the projection from 138,639 neuron rates to the 73 observed channels is a fixed synapse-count-weighted average with NO free parameters. WHAT WAS FITTED: the ~1.5e7 synaptic weight magnitudes |w_ij|, the 138,639 per-neuron time constants tau_i, the 138,639 per-neuron initial rates r_i(0), and two global drive parameters (noise scale s, offset mu). First-order rate equation with ReLU, integrated by exponential Euler at dt = 1/1.2 s = 833 ms, matched to the imaging rate; trained by BPTT with gradient checkpointing (and reproduced with BrainTrace online gradients), Adam lr 1e-3, batch 64, on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 Pro GPU.
- VALIDATION AND CONTROLS: first half of the ~14-min trace for training, second half held out (no separate validation split; training stopped at loss plateau). Held-out functional-connectivity agreement r = 0.423 for the connectome model versus a ceiling of r = 0.473 set by the recording's own train-test FC drift, and r = 0.288 for a parameter-matched GRU (train-set FC was r = 0.994 vs 0.941). Intact model on held-out data: loss ~0.25, Pearson r ~0.8 against the empirical neuropil traces. Three unfitted features are reported as agreeing with independent experiment: truncated-lognormal weight distribution (mu = -4.97, sigma = 1.83, KS = 0.06), scale-free avalanches, and shortened time constants in fast visual cells. Structural controls are parameter-matched GRU and MLP baselines and a freely learned linear readout replacing the anatomical one. NOTE FOR OUR CONTRACT: there is NO shuffled- or randomised-connectome control anywhere in the main text or the 26-page supplement ('shuffl*' and 'randomi*' return zero hits in both).
- THE TIME-CONSTANT RESULT, PRECISELY: tau_i were initialised by independent draws from a uniform distribution (range not stated) and refined; 'Training left most of the 138,639 time constants near their starting values, but moved a minority sharply in both directions.' The reported result is the sign and cell-type identity of the two 3% tails of delta_tau = tau_post - tau_init, not calibrated tau values. The shortened tail is enriched for exactly the cells this project measures: lamina monopolar L1 (~28% of the tail, about two-thirds of all L1 cells in the brain), L5, centrifugal C2 and C3, motion detectors T4 and T5, and their medulla inputs Mi9 and Tm5c; plus ellipsoid-body ring neurons and Delta7. No absolute tau values, ranges or units are given in the main text or the supplement I retrieved.
- THE AUTHORS' OWN IDENTIFIABILITY LIMITS (Supplementary Note 4, titled only about the weights): 'individual per-synapse magnitudes are not separately identified'; 'the absolute scale is conventional, fixed by rescaling the deconvolved target to a mean rate of 1 Hz, so only relative weights are interpretable'; reproducibility is demonstrated only at neuropil-aggregate (+/-5% across priors and seeds), distributional and cluster levels; 'all main-text fits come from one fly, so the cross-model agreement measures fitting variability, not biological variability. Identifiability across animals is untested.' No identifiability analysis is offered for the 138,639 per-neuron time constants at all.
- RELEASED ARTEFACTS FOR THIS PREPRINT: none of the fitted parameters. Data availability points only to the source calcium recordings (figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.13349282); 'the preprocessed data and the trained model weights will be made available upon acceptance'. Code availability: built on the open BrainX ecosystem (https://github.com/chaobrain/brainx) and 'will be made available upon acceptance'. So as of 2026-09-18 there is no downloadable weight or time-constant table from this paper.
- A SECOND, PEER-REVIEWED PAPER FROM THE SAME GROUP DOES HAVE RELEASED CODE, BUT FITS LESS: Wang et al., 'Model-agnostic linear-memory online learning in spiking neural networks', Nature Communications 17:1745, published 2026-01-19 (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-68453-w). Its Fig. 6 trains a FlyWire-constrained whole-brain SPIKING network (>125,000 neurons, ~50 million synapses, LIF dynamics taken from Shiu et al. 2024) to reproduce the same Mann/Turner resting-state calcium data (17 min, 68 ROIs, 80/20 split, teacher-forced next-step prediction at 1.2 Hz with a 0.2 ms simulation step, 4165 steps per sample). Crucially, the biophysical time constants there are FIXED and UNIFORM for every neuron - T_mbr = 20 ms, tau_syn = 5 ms, T_refractory = 2.2 ms, T_delay = 1.8 ms, V_rest = V_reset = -52 mV, V_th = -45 mV, W_syn = 0.275 mV - and only synaptic strength plus a GRU input encoder are trained. In the released code (github.com/chaobrain/fitting_drosophila_whole_brain_spiking_model, pushed 2026-01-19) the trainable synaptic term is a rank-20 LoRA added to the fixed connectome-scaled weights ('--n_rank', default 20; forward pass is conn(pre_spk)*scale_factor + lora(pre_spk)), with tau_m = 20 ms and tau_syn = 5 ms hard-coded defaults. The repo ships code and a Google-Drive link to preprocessed data, but no trained checkpoints and no LICENSE file.
- FOR CONTRAST, WHAT FLYVIS ACTUALLY FITS (verified in the Nature paper): 45,669 neurons, 1,513,231 connections, 64 cell types, 721 columns, and just 734 free parameters - one time constant and one resting potential per cell type, plus one unitary synapse strength per cell-type-to-cell-type connection; synapse counts set relative weights within a type pair, and signs come from neurotransmitter/receptor profiling. Those 734 parameters are optimised end-to-end on optic-flow estimation from rendered Sintel sequences, with no neural activity used in fitting; activity enters only as post-hoc validation.
- NEAREST RELEVANT THEORY AND CAUTIONS: Beiran & Litwin-Kumar (Nature Neuroscience, published 2025-10-27, DOI 10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4) show formally that a connectome is often insufficient to constrain the dynamics of a task-performing network, but that recordings from a small subset of neurons can remove the degeneracy - and their framework prioritises WHICH neurons to record. Karaneen, Schomburg & Chklovskii (bioRxiv 2026.04.18.717873, 2026-04-20) retrain flyvis-style ensembles and report that task error does not reliably select the biologically plausible solution in the Drosophila motion pathway. Zhou & Hasler (bioRxiv 2026.06.10.731214, 2026-06-12) probe the pretrained flyvis ensemble against sign-preserving weight-shuffle baselines rejection-sampled for stability - a control design directly usable in our setting. All three argue against treating any single fitted parameter set as the truth.
- NO BETTER-MATCHING CANDIDATE EXISTS. Systematic Europe PMC queries over 2026 preprints and articles (FlyWire in abstract; connectome+Drosophila; calcium+connectome+whole-brain; whole-brain model+fly; connectome-constrained) return exactly one August 2026 item matching the claim - the Li et al. preprint above. The arXiv API over abs:connectome AND abs:Drosophila returns nothing in August 2026 (nearest: arXiv 2609.01330, 'Connectome-Based Modelling Reveals Orientation Maps in the Drosophila Optic Lobe', 2026-09-01, which I did not examine). Other 2026 fly-connectome items found (NeuroGraphBench 10.64898/2026.08.22.746456; 'Connectome analysis reveals brainwide visual processing in Drosophila' 10.64898/2026.02.02.700492; arXiv 2602.17997 locomotion control) do not fit recordings.

## applied_recommendation

Do not transplant time constants or synaptic strengths from this work; it cannot be done today and would not be better if it could. Three independent blocks. (1) AVAILABILITY: the preprint releases nothing - weights and preprocessed data are promised 'upon acceptance', and the only released fly-fitting artefact from this group (the Nature Communications BrainTrace repo) holds code with hard-coded uniform tau_m = 20 ms / tau_syn = 5 ms and a rank-20 LoRA weight adaptation, i.e. no per-cell-type time constants and no free per-synapse strengths to take. The preprint is also CC BY-NC-ND (no derivatives) and the code repo carries no licence. (2) TEMPORAL RESOLUTION: the fit's only evidence is 73 region-averaged traces sampled at 1.2 Hz and smoothed by a GCaMP6s kernel the authors themselves describe as an approximately 10-second low-pass filter, integrated at dt = 833 ms. The per-cell-type constants this project needs for L1/L5/Mi/Tm/T4/T5 live in the 10-100 ms range; a target with essentially no power above ~0.1 Hz cannot identify them. Their own visual-cell result is reported as a rank claim (which 3% of neurons shortened relative to a random uniform initialisation), never as a value. (3) IDENTIFIABILITY: Supplementary Note 4 states that individual per-synapse magnitudes are not separately identified, that the absolute weight scale is conventional (target rescaled to mean 1 Hz) so only relative weights are interpretable, that reproducibility holds only at neuropil-aggregate/distributional/cluster level, and that all main-text fits come from one fly with cross-animal identifiability untested - and it offers no identifiability analysis for the 138,639 time constants at all. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE for a recordings-fitted model to beat the flyvis transplant: (a) the recordings must resolve the cells whose parameters you want, at their own timescale - cell-type-identified optic-lobe imaging or ephys at tens of Hz or better, not region averages (this is exactly Beiran & Litwin-Kumar's result: recordings remove degeneracy only where you record); (b) the per-cell-type tau must be shown identifiable - recovered consistently across seeds, priors and animals, and demonstrably moved away from initialisation rather than left near it; (c) weights must come with a stated physical or explicitly transferable normalisation, not a conventional 1 Hz rescale; (d) parameters must be released keyed to neuron IDs, and mapped onto MaleCNS through a mapping we verify ourselves (FlyWire v783 is a different animal and a different connectome from our canonical one); (e) a shuffled-connectivity control must exist in the source, which it does not here. USE IT INSTEAD AS A CITABLE QUALITATIVE CROSS-CHECK: an independent whole-brain fit, with no visual task and no stimulus, pushed the shortest timescales onto L1 (about two-thirds of all L1 cells), L5, C2, C3, Mi9, Tm5c, T4 and T5 - the same early-visual chain where our luminance decodability is highest (photoreceptors 1.000, lamina 0.713) and which degrades by T4/T5 (0.366). Cite it as convergent evidence about the ordering of timescales along the pathway, never as a parameter source. If we want measured per-cell-type time constants rather than task-derived ones, the route is the primary optic-lobe physiology literature that flyvis was validated against, which is a separate research request.
